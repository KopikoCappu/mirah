How it decides

Missing something important costs more than reading one extra email, so Mirah leans towards Important on purpose.

  1. 1

    Your rules come first

    VIP senders, companies you've applied to, hiring systems like Greenhouse and Lever, keywords like “interview” or “offer letter”, and anyone you've emailed before go straight to Important. Blocked senders go to Junk.

  2. 2

    Then one quick question to AI

    Everything else goes to Jev, a fast, inexpensive classifier. In a single call it works out the category, whether you need to act, how urgent it is, and whether it looks like a scam.

  3. 3

    Cautious sorting

    Anything with even a 15% chance of being job-related goes to Important. Junk only gets an email when Jev is at least 90% sure and nothing needs you. If Jev isn't sure, or has an error, the email goes to Review.

Built to be trusted with your inbox

Private by design

You run your own copy, with your own database and your own API key. Your email never passes through anyone else's server.

Never deletes

Mirah only reads and sorts. It can add Mirah/* labels in Gmail or categories in Outlook, and that's it.

Learns from you

Move an email with one tap, and Mirah remembers that sender next time.

Shows its accuracy

An Accuracy page tracks every correction: what it missed, and what it wrongly sent to Junk.

On your phone

Install it to your home screen from Safari or Chrome. It works like an app, without an app store.

Free to run

At personal volume, it fits in Google Cloud's and Vercel's free tiers. You only pay Jev for classifying email, a fraction of a cent each.

Run your own

One script asks three questions, then sets up your database and deploys to Vercel or Cloud Run. It hands you a private link to finish in the browser, where a step-by-step wizard walks you through Google sign-in and your Jev key.

$ git clone https://github.com/KopikoCappu/mirah.git
$ cd mirah
$ npm install
$ bash scripts/setup.sh

You'll need: a Google account, Node.js, the gcloud CLI, and a Jev API key. On Windows, run it in Git Bash. Prefer to do it by hand? Read SETUP.md.

Questions

Why do I have to host it myself?

Reading Gmail is a “restricted” permission. A shared hosted service would need Google's paid security audit, and would have to hold thousands of people's email. With your own copy, your mail stays in your own account.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You need to be comfortable pasting commands into a terminal. The setup script tells you what to click, and your deployment's /setup page lists anything that's still missing.

Will Google show a warning?

Yes, once. Since it's your own personal app, Google says it “hasn't verified this app”. You click through it one time, and the connection keeps working.

What does Jev see?

The sender, subject, and the first part of each new email that your rules didn't already sort. Full email bodies aren't stored anywhere.
